What we would pitch

A preferred pair, with useful alternatives.

Uptown: Clara, with a quieter exit

Follow Harmony and Liberty to Clara, then leave on Joseph for South Robertson and Calhoun. This removes about 306 metres of State Street riding, the extra university Freret section and the Palmer walking connection from the earlier proposal.

The remaining advantage over the Robertson route is mostly its crossing pattern: more supported all-way junctions and fewer ordinary stops. Its measured pavement is only modestly smoother. Keep Robertson as the simpler alternative.

Downtown: keep OCH, turn on Clio

Keep the Loyola → Octavia → LaSalle → Valence → Loyola jog, then Foucher → Saratoga → First and the Danneel/Philip connection to OCH. Clio provides the final transfer to Carondelet and Lafayette.

The Clio finish adds about 119 metres but removes the dated O’Keefe/Girod temporary-control concern. Clio has stops at Baronne and Carondelet; both cross streets have evidence of an uncontrolled approach, so these are ordinary stops, not all-way junctions.

Baronne or Danneel to Loyola

The Baronne option stays on Baronne to Amelia, then joins Loyola. The Danneel option uses OCH → Philip → Danneel → Amelia → Loyola. Both reach campus through Palmer and LaSalle Place, then use the same campus and Maple finish.

Danneel direct models at 22.5 minutes, versus 23.3 for Baronne, with a lower measured pavement index (2.88 versus 3.42). The smoother Danneel / LaSalle jog takes Valence → LaSalle → Joseph back to Loyola: about 69 model seconds longer, bringing its pavement index down to 2.55. Clara remains smoother at 2.44. The timing model does not convert that pavement difference into extra minutes.

What the time estimates do—and do not—mean

These are estimates for one rider using the same e-bike and usually similar assistance. “Ride short links” follows that rider’s requested timing scenario; it does not change mapped permissions. “Walk short links” uses 1.2 m/s on designated links. Both assume 3 m/s on campus paths and omit unsurveyed access and crossing delay. Differences of a few tens of seconds are not reliable wins.

306 rides, plus a closer look at crossings

A stop sign and a full stop
are different measurements.

The broad timing model predicts average recorded behaviour. The follow-up analysis compares equal 120-metre sections on the same ride and street, matching direction, time and pavement.

An ordinary sign encounter added about 3.7 seconds on average, including passages without a recorded stop. In the nine qualifying cases with a clear stop or near-stop, the average addition was 16.7 seconds.

That includes slowing, waiting and acceleration inside the section. It is not just stationary time. The small stopped sample and possible acceleration beyond the window limit precision.

Crossing two-direction traffic took longer: ordinary STOP passages averaged +4.4 seconds versus +1.8 seconds crossing verified one-way streets. Traffic, width and layout also differ, so this is an association rather than a causal two-way penalty.

A bit of methodology

Four kinds of evidence,
kept distinct.

Street geometry and local knowledge

OpenStreetMap supplies connected streets and mapped directions. Bike Streets supplies the community network. Local feedback excludes State, longitudinal Napoleon and Louisiana, most Freret, Poydras and parking-aisle shortcuts; it also flags wide Marengo.

Measured pavement

34,004 Bumpy Roads cells represent 145,712 readings. Samples are matched to nearby streets and weighted by distance. Missing pavement stays missing; repeated rides on one block do not make that block count more in the route average.

Directional control evidence

Mapillary detections are checked against geometry and imagery, Google Street View and rider observations. To establish all-way status, we look at the other approaches—not just for a plaque. Sources and dates remain attached to the map markers.

Personal timing, separate from comfort

The main fit uses 1,433 suitable sections from 176 rides on 95 days, tested on held-out days. Roughness and stop annoyance remain explicit preferences rather than invented extra minutes. An all-way comfort benefit never erases its physical time cost.

How this compares with taking St. Charles

The original sensitivity analysis put St. Charles near 21½ minutes versus about 23½ minutes for the then-fastest quiet routes, using the fitted road pace and roughly 3.4 seconds per signal encounter. At 8–12 seconds per signal, much of that difference disappears or reverses. Sustaining the observed 31 km/h on two short stretches would make St. Charles much faster, but that was not demonstrated for the full corridor. Many St. Charles approaches remain unresolved. These scenarios exclude construction delay; they are not a proven whole-route prediction.

Evidence limits

GPS is typically recorded around once per second, with median reported accuracy near 3 metres. The controlled stop comparison has only nine clear stopping cases. Joseph is treated as having no STOP at Magnolia, following the rider’s acceptance of the two March 2016 Mapillary views. Pavement timestamps are absent from public tiles, and observations are not reliably lane-specific. Quietness uses community classification and local knowledge, not measured traffic counts or a crash-risk model. The original comparison contained 69 geometries, with 16 meeting its prior screen; the current State-free shortlist is a revision of that work, not proof of a citywide optimum.

The practical proposal

Complete the connections.
Make the route understandable.

Pitch a coherent boulevard corridor with specific work at the remaining interruptions. A line on the map is the starting point for that work.

  • Clara / SoniatCheck the actual pedestrian crossing and short offset. The existing walk geometry does not survey the entire maneuver.
  • First / Danneel / PhilipResolve the short connection to OCH with a comfortable, lawful design; keep the walking treatment visible until then.
  • Campus access and surfaceConfirm permitted access, hours and pedestrian expectations. Measure the unrecorded paths, including the mapped gravel section.
  • Clio pavementThe 275-metre Clio connection is on Bike Streets, but has no Bumpy Roads measurements in the snapshot. Record it before promising a smooth finish.
  • Major-road crossingsReview Napoleon, Louisiana, Freret and Carrollton in the field. The model does not establish warrants for new traffic controls.
  • Publish the evidence, then test the corridorReview the saved control database for OSM contribution, check how Bike Streets uses the tags, and repeat paired end-to-end rides at different times of day.
